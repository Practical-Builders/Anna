About

ColorSeen was born from frustration.

As someone with color vision deficiency, I found myself constantly asking friends and family, "What color is this really?" Online shopping became an exercise in guesswork. Marketing names like "Sunset Shimmer" and "Spiced Honey" told me nothing about the actual color I was buying.

I wasn't asking for much — just a clear, honest description. But that simple need wasn't being met anywhere.

So I built ColorSeen.

❀

Why This Matters

Over 300 million people worldwide experience color vision deficiency. Millions more are visually impaired or blind. Yet the online shopping experience is designed as if everyone perceives color the same way.

The fashion and beauty industries have made this worse, not better. What started as creative color naming has evolved into a barrier. For example, "Nude" can be so many different colors.

For sighted shoppers, these names are charming marketing. For people who can't see colors clearly, they're obstacles to independence.

ColorSeen was developed out of a genuine experience of being unable to shop online independently. The Chrome extension is designed by someone who understands that struggle and wanted to fill the gap between marketing language and understandable colors.

❀

What We Believe

Shopping should be accessible to everyone.

Color descriptions shouldn't require a decoder ring. You shouldn't need to ask someone else what "Bordeaux Nights" means. And you definitely shouldn't have to buy three shades just to find one that works.

  • Clear communication is better than clever marketing
  • Accessibility isn't a nice-to-have — it's essential
  • Technology should empower independence, not create more barriers
  • You deserve to shop with confidence

ColorSeen exists to make online shopping accessible, independent, and frustration-free.
